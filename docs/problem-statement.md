# Problem statement

Number portability is the critical path of almost every enterprise voice
migration. The dates are set by regulation and by the losing carrier, not
by the project, and they do not move without cost.

Today the work is tracked in a spreadsheet that one person maintains, plus
the email thread with the carrier, plus that person's memory.

## What goes wrong

| Problem | Consequence |
|---|---|
| An overdue LOA is noticed late | The range misses its window and the whole wave slips by up to 45 days |
| A range is submitted with an invalid or duplicated number | The carrier rejects the LOA, costing 5 to 10 days on the critical path |
| Cutover dates live in one person's calendar | The voice engineer prepares the wrong range on the wrong night |
| Status is reported from a spreadsheet snapshot | The weekly report is already outdated when it is sent |
| No history of who changed what | Post-incident review cannot reconstruct the sequence |

## Why now

Migrations have grown from single-site to multi-city programs. A single
program can carry 40 or more ranges across three cutover nights. The
spreadsheet that worked for four ranges does not survive forty.
